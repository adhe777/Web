Flowers are the highly evolved reproductive structures of angiosperms (flowering plants) that facilitate pollination, produce seeds, and ensure the survival of plant species. They are composed of four main parts: the colorful petals that attract pollinators, the protective sepals at the base, the male stamen which generates pollen, and the female pistil where fertilization occurs and seeds develop. To reproduce, flowers rely on either biotic pollination (using insects, birds, or bats attracted by scent and nectar) or abiiotic pollination (using wind or water currents to scatter light pollen). In landscaping and agriculture, they are categorized by their lifespans into annuals, which complete their life cycle in a single year; perennials, which return year after year; and biennials, which require two years to bloom and seed. Beyond their beauty and cultural symbolism, flowers are vital to human industry, serving as essential components in medicine, culinary arts, herbal teas, and the global perfume trade.
